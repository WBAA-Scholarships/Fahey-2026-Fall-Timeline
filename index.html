Reviewer Guidelines

How we keep scoring fair and consistent

Questions? Email scholarships@willaflight.org.

Do

What to do
  • Score only what is written in the application. No assumptions.
  • Score each application on your own before comparing notes with anyone
  • Consider the whole applicant, not just one strong or weak area
  • Jot down a quick reason when you give a very high or very low score
  • Email scholarships@willaflight.org if you are unsure how to score a particular situation
  • Remember the goal: find candidates who will make the most of this opportunity

Don't

What to avoid
  • Talk about scores or rankings with other reviewers before you've turned in your own
  • Let one very strong or very weak area pull up or drag down unrelated scores
  • Make assumptions about need, background, or identity beyond what's written
  • Favor or penalize a particular school, career path, or writing style
  • Share any applicant information outside the committee
  • Score an applicant you know personally. Recuse yourself instead.

Bias to Watch For

Keep in mind
  • Prestige bias: Going to a well-known school doesn’t make someone a stronger candidate
  • Extracurricular bias: Students who help support their family may have fewer activities, so look at quality over quantity
  • Writing style bias: English may not be their first language, so judge the ideas, not the polish
  • Recency bias: Don’t let a strong last paragraph make up for a weak application overall
  • When in doubt, ask yourself: "Am I scoring the application, or my assumptions about the person?"
